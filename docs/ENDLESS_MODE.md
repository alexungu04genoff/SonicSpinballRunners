# Endless authored-Act experiment

Open **Play Endless.cmd**. This is another level source for the shared game loop, ball, input, profiles, mechanics and camera. Free Play remains the default application mode.

The trial uses checked-in snapshots of **Second Simple**, **Third Complex**, and **First Try Smooth Decor** in `data/endless/`. Each Zone contains three shuffled Acts under one theme. Themes use a shuffled bag without adjacent repeats. Act entry and exit connectors align physically; crossing the next Act's activation line transfers the checkpoint. No score gate, limited lives or new character physics is introduced.

## Continuous travel

Normal play never changes scenes or teleports between Acts. Upcoming collision and visual roots attach above the existing course. The same camera follows the same ball across the seam. Draining retries the active Act's Spawn.

The background transition starts 200 pixels before a Zone entrance and finishes 250 pixels beyond it. These distances are provisional. Two independently rendered scenery/sky layers follow the gameplay camera during the overlap. Scenery fades by camera travel distance; foreground light direction, color, intensity, ambient color and exposure blend with it. Old scenery stops rendering after the fade and retires later. Foreground lighting uses ambient color without live sky reflections so changing the directional light does not repeatedly rebuild sky radiance. Reflective-material polish remains a visual-review item.

**T** starts a spectator camera tour with physics parked. It never pushes the ball into geometry. **N** jumps the camera to the next ready Act only in this diagnostic tour. Ordinary traversal does not use that jump. Leaving the tour serves safely at the current checkpoint. The HUD provides tour speed, pause and reset; **F3** shows streaming timings, disk-loaded Acts/themes, and runtime rebuild counts.

## Preparation and bounds

Startup waits for the first **two** Act/theme variants and the opening background. It does not enumerate every Act/theme combination. Once play begins, preparation fills the rest of the current Zone and the next Zone. Only one library preparation request runs at a time.

The library keeps at most eight visual recipes and three theme definitions, with active entries pinned. Resident scenes retain the previous Act and a bounded lookahead. Full bakes load asynchronously and bypass procedural terrain, prop and background generation, theme constructors and scene capture. Each live physics shape and visual node is installed separately under a frame budget. Old trees are freed incrementally. Missing or stale bakes stop with an offline-rebake message. The explicit developer fallback runs private resource calculations on workers; scene nodes remain on the main thread. A private viewport prepares graphics in batches before readiness. Equivalent material colors no longer each cost a separate warm-up frame. If the player outruns preparation, physics pauses safely before an unavailable entrance with the course still visible, then resumes automatically.

First-use graphics setup includes uploading textures and meshes to the graphics card and compiling shaders. Godot Compatibility/ANGLE can still stall during these operations. Full bakes remove procedural scene generation, but resource loading, scene instantiation and GPU work remain. Driver shader caches are machine-specific; a portable scene bake cannot replace them. Godot's export shader baker does not support Compatibility ([official documentation](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/performance/pipeline_compilations.html)). Frame timing remains an explicit prototype check; this implementation does not guarantee hitch-free cold loading on every machine.

## Bake after verifying an Act

1. Finish playtesting and save the Act in Act Builder.
2. Click **Bake** in the editor header. A separate progress window prepares complete visual recipes for every theme.
3. Keep the resulting `<Act name>.bake/` folder beside its `.tres` source, plus the shared `data/endless/themes.bake/` and `data/endless/assets.bake/` folders.

Version 3 sidecars store pre-decomposed collision shapes, terrain meshes, props, material assignments and one-node visual recipes with explicit animation bindings. These recipes contain no live gameplay state. Each theme background, sky, lighting setup and foreground material preload list is saved once in `themes.bake`, independent of the number of Acts. Primitive meshes are converted to vertex buffers offline. Identical meshes and procedural image textures use content-addressed files in `assets.bake`; imported assets retain their external references. All Acts reuse those resources instead of recalculating or embedding another copy in each sidecar.

Every sidecar records its source fingerprint, engine version and presentation-pipeline fingerprint, including referenced generators, shaders and assets. Hashing runs off the main thread and is reused within a session. Earlier sidecar versions are stale and must be regenerated. Source Acts are never rewritten. Re-bake after changing an Act, generator or referenced art. Sidecars are derived artifacts ignored by Git; packaging a release must include freshly generated Act sidecars and both shared folders. Normal startup requires valid full bakes and explains how to regenerate missing or stale files; it never silently switches to procedural rebuilding. The explicit developer `--no-bakes` flag still tests that fallback. There is no startup sweep over Act/theme combinations and no automatic disk bake during gameplay.

Once the immediate playable neighbor is ready, the next Zone gets a separate theme-only request. It loads the shared sky, scenery, lighting and material list without opening an Act sidecar or compiling physics. Small box probes prepare foreground texture and shader features, including shadows; scenery warms separately. Repeated uniform colors share a probe, while distinct textures, shaders, feature flags and next passes remain represented. The background mounts incrementally while still invisible. Act recipes retain their own later preparation because materials alone cannot prepare every mesh layout or particle shader. During play, warm-up draws at most eight geometry instances per batch; startup allows 64. Both stop at the first new shader feature. The existing three-theme and eight-variant cache limits remain in force. F3 and `ENDLESS_THEME_PREFETCH` report independent theme preparation; `source=disk` and `geometry generated=0` distinguish loading from offline baking.

Low-poly visual baking simplifies final terrain, floor and rail contours with a maximum 0.35 local-pixel error per contour. It preserves endpoints and turns of at least 25 degrees, rejects crossings, and retains unsafe/self-touching input unchanged. Wall caps and trim use the same reduced outline. It also caps high-detail round primitives (16 radial segments; sphere 7 rings, cylinder 1 ring, capsule 4 rings; torus 20 by 8). Existing lower-detail props remain unchanged. Source Acts and collision contours retain their exact samples. The 50 px J-rail fixture, including its cap border, drops from 969 to 310 triangles; this is a representative fixture, not a whole-Act reduction claim.

This detail policy applies to all newly baked Endless variants. Standalone Act mode retains its existing visual generation. A J-rail comparison produced 310 triangles at 0.35, 0.5 and 0.75 px tolerances, 233 at 1 px, and 226 at 1.5 or 2 px. The default remains 0.35 px: further trim-mesh reduction and spatial static batching are candidates for a separate experiment, rather than automatically increasing visible collision/mesh mismatch. Other possible bake additions are distance detail levels and independently loadable sections within a large authored Act; none are implemented by this change.

Endless uses the existing gameplay N64-ish + VHS + CRT defaults and F4 / Shift+F4 controls. Both overlapping backgrounds and the foreground are composed before the shader stack; the HUD remains at native resolution. Theme surface, water, foliage and animation shaders remain part of the baked material resources.

**Bake Endless.cmd** prepares the three trial snapshots in one offline run. This is the authoring bake, not a session loading screen. The original editable Acts in `data/acts/` remain separate from those snapshots. Replacing a trial snapshot requires re-baking it.

For a repeatable run, use `Play Endless.cmd -Seed 18 -Tour -FrameTiming`. The pool currently contains the three agreed Acts; adding a larger authored library requires updating the pool manifest and verifying connector compatibility.

## Checks

- `tests/endless_regression.gd`: seeded ordering, connector alignment, all nine ordered seams and source immutability.
- `tests/geometry_bake_regression.gd`: round-trip collision equality, stale-bake rejection and material-free mesh serialization.
- `tests/endless_cache_regression.gd`: complete recipe serialization, animation rebinding, shared external textures and warm-up feature signatures.
- `tests/endless_detail_regression.gd`: all terrain stamps at three scales, contour error limits, sharp notches, J-rail triangle counts and primitive detail/source preservation.
- `tests/endless_scene_smoke.gd`: startup scope, readiness guard, bounded caches, nine Zone transitions, stationary tour ball, shared engine identities, background blending, physical checkpoint activation without teleporting and normal launch/reset. `--captures` writes screenshots; `--transitions=3` runs a short rendered check; `--full-buffer` also waits for six resident Acts; `--require-bakes` fails on procedural fallback; `--no-bakes` exercises the fallback explicitly.

The initial streaming implementation passed ordering/seams (788 checks), collision serialization (1,001), themes (813), and editor smoke (92). Its unbaked rendered opening buffer took 26.505 seconds in one run. The full disk-cache implementation has separate verification below; timings are local diagnostic observations, not performance guarantees. The existing mechanics regression's composed spinner/magnet/rail fixture also failed on unchanged commit `b5fe20b`.

Full-cache verification on Godot 4.7.2 / Compatibility / ANGLE: the rendered nine-Zone smoke passed 218 checks with every requested variant loaded from disk and zero runtime rebuilds. Startup took 5.461 seconds and the largest measured frame was 73.00 ms. A separate continuous camera tour at the default speed reached the third Zone with zero entrance-wait time and zero rebuilds; its largest frame was 119.52 ms. Driver caches were already warm, so these measurements do not establish first-launch performance. A clean checkout containing only the intended code changes passed cache serialization/sharing checks (20), collision serialization (1,001), and the explicitly unbaked fallback smoke (43). The nine-Zone teardown reported two zero-reference ObjectDB instances at process exit; the continuous tour and clean fallback did not report that warning.

Manual review: pinball traversal and flipper timing, connector readability, scenery/lighting at the blend midpoint, and cold-frame performance. Unlimited-duration coordinate rebasing and production packaging remain future work.

Theme-prefetch/low-poly verification: terrain detail checks passed 939 assertions, collision round-trip 1,001, seam/order 788, and cache/probe checks 24. A shader-enabled nine-Zone diagnostic-jump run passed 225 checks, including bounded caches and a theme-only request that left Act recipes, physics templates and resident Acts unchanged. Its largest frame was 549 ms during first-use theme preparation, so prefetching does not eliminate all device-side stalls. A continuous camera tour at 100 px/s reached all eight themes with seven theme-only preloads, zero entrance waits and zero runtime rebuilds. Its largest blend frame was 72 ms and largest overall frame 157.29 ms; driver caches were already warm. This is a spectator test, not manual pinball playtesting. The existing two-instance ObjectDB teardown warning remains. An isolated checkout without the other art conversation's changes passed detail (939), cache/probe (24) and explicit fallback startup/transitions (46). All 24 local Act/theme bakes passed the fingerprint audit. Deduplicating theme probes reduced Green Hill's list from 550 to 15 and the other themes to 13–17 entries without dropping distinct shader/texture combinations.

After probe deduplication, the rendered short smoke passed 57 checks (5.82 s opening load, 29.81 ms largest frame). A subsequent continuous tour through three Zones still had zero entrance waits/rebuilds, but caught a 944.17 ms frame while the next Green Hill Act was warming during Zone 1. This variation prevents a claim of consistently smooth streaming; the smaller smoke timing is not a performance guarantee. Further profiling should separate graphics/driver work from resource loading and evaluate offline static batching and simpler trim. The 0.35 px contour tolerance remains unchanged.
